CHILDREN & TEENS

The child who says “I don’t care.”

Sometimes the words that sound like indifference are asking a much harder question: Is it safe to care out loud?

“I don’t care.” Three words that can bring a conversation to a stop. For a parent, they may sound dismissive. For a young person, they can sometimes feel like armor.

Not every shrug hides pain. Sometimes a child genuinely isn't interested. But when “I don’t care” appears right after disappointment, embarrassment, or conflict, curiosity may take us further than correction.

Instead of demanding an explanation in the moment, try: “You don’t have to talk about it right now. I’m here when you want to.” Then make room for the conversation to happen later, without an audience or a deadline.

Connection doesn’t mean abandoning boundaries. It means remembering that the person behind the behavior still needs to feel seen.

Every story deserves to be heard.
