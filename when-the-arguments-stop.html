RELATIONSHIPS

When the arguments stop, but the distance grows.

Silence can look like peace from the outside. Inside a relationship, it can tell a different story.

People often think the worst part of a struggling relationship is the fighting. Sometimes it’s what happens when two people stop trying to be understood.

The house may be quieter. The daily routines may continue. But the small invitations disappear: a story from the day, a hand on the shoulder, the question “What’s really going on with you?”

Quiet doesn’t always mean disconnection, and conflict isn’t proof that a relationship is failing. What matters is whether both people still have room to be honest and feel safe doing so.

One place to begin is small: “I miss feeling close to you. Could we talk for ten minutes without trying to solve everything?” Rebuilding connection is rarely one grand conversation. More often, it’s the decision to keep showing up for the next one.

Every story deserves to be heard.
